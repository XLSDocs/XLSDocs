Someone copies a working SUMIF formula, adds a second condition, and
the total comes back wrong — not an error, just quietly incorrect.
Nine times out of ten, the culprit is the same thing: SUMIF and
SUMIFS put `sum_range` in a different position, and Excel won't warn
you when you get it wrong.

## The argument order actually flips

<ExcelCode>{`=SUMIF(range, criteria, [sum_range])
=SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, ...)`}</ExcelCode>

SUMIF puts `sum_range` **last and optional** — if it's missing,
SUMIF just sums `range` itself. SUMIFS puts it **first and
required**. Upgrading a SUMIF to a SUMIFS by literally adding
`,S` and a second condition, without re-ordering the arguments, is
the single most common way this breaks.

<Callout type="warn">
  `SUMIF(A2:A10, "West", B2:B10)` and `SUMIFS(B2:B10, A2:A10,
  "West")` return the same result — but they are not
  interchangeable by just adding an S. Swapping formula types means
  re-checking every argument's position, not just appending a
  condition.
</Callout>

## Why this specific mistake is so easy to make

Both functions look almost identical at a glance, and both are
genuinely doing "sum this column where that column matches." The
brain pattern-matches on function name and general shape, not
argument position — especially when editing a formula that already
works, under time pressure, with the goal of "just add one more
condition."

## The one-condition shortcut that avoids the whole problem

If a formula is only ever going to need one condition, SUMIF's
optional third argument has a shortcut worth knowing: when the
column being summed *is* the column being tested, skip `sum_range`
entirely.

<ExcelCode>{`=SUMIF(Sales[Amount], ">1000")`}</ExcelCode>

No `sum_range`, because `range` and the values to total are the same
column. This only works for SUMIF, not SUMIFS — COUNTIFS-family
functions never infer a missing sum range.

## A rule of thumb that actually holds

Reach for SUMIF only when a formula is genuinely one condition, one
time, and unlikely to grow. The moment a second condition shows up —
which happens more often than the first draft of a spreadsheet
expects — switch to SUMIFS and rebuild the argument order from
scratch, rather than patching the SUMIF call in place. It takes the
same amount of typing either way, and it removes the exact failure
mode that produces a total that's wrong without ever throwing an
error.
