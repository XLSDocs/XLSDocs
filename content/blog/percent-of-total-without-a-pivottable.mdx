"What percent of the total does this row represent?" is one of the
most common questions a spreadsheet gets asked, and until recently
Excel answered it in exactly two ways: a PivotTable's "Show Values
As → % of Grand Total" setting, or a manual `SUM(subset)/SUM(total)`
formula copied down a column with the denominator locked by `$`.
Both still work. Three newer functions now cover the same ground
directly in a formula, without a separate PivotTable object to
maintain.

## The manual way, and why it's easy to get subtly wrong

<ExcelCode>{`=SUM(C5)/SUM($C$5:$C$12)`}</ExcelCode>

This is correct, and it's also exactly as fragile as any formula
that depends on a human remembering to lock the right reference with
`$`. Copy it down without the absolute reference on the denominator,
and every row silently divides by the wrong range instead of
throwing an error — a wrong percentage that looks entirely
plausible.

## PERCENTOF: the same formula, with the easy mistake removed

<ExcelCode>{`=PERCENTOF(data_subset, data_all)`}</ExcelCode>

[PERCENTOF](/docs/math/percentof) is logically identical to the
`SUM/SUM` pattern above — it still needs the denominator argument
locked with `$` when copied down — but it reads as what it actually
means, instead of as two sums that happen to be divided:

<ExcelCode>{`=PERCENTOF(C5, $C$5:$C$12)`}</ExcelCode>

It doesn't require `data_subset` to be a literal part of `data_all`
either — it's just two sums divided — so it works just as well on
two unrelated ranges, though "this row's share of a total it belongs
to" is by far the most common use.

## Where it actually saves work: inside GROUPBY

A single row's share of a total is one formula. A **category's**
share of a total, for every category at once, is where this used to
mean a PivotTable. [GROUPBY](/docs/lookup/groupby) takes PERCENTOF
directly as its aggregation function:

<ExcelCode>{`=GROUPBY(A2:A76, D2:D76, SUM) → total sales per region`}</ExcelCode>

<ExcelCode>{`=GROUPBY(A2:A76, D2:D76, PERCENTOF) → each region's share of the overall total`}</ExcelCode>

Same grouping column, same values column — swapping `SUM` for
`PERCENTOF` is the entire difference between a totals table and a
"% of total" table. Both spill live from source data, with no
refresh button and no separate query object sitting alongside the
sheet.

## Two dimensions: PIVOTBY and relative_to

[PIVOTBY](/docs/lookup/pivotby) is GROUPBY's two-dimensional sibling
— rows and columns both, the shape a PivotTable produces with a row
field and a column field assigned. Its `relative_to` argument
controls what each cell's percentage is measured against, which is
exactly the setting a PivotTable buries a few clicks deep in "Show
Values As":

<ExcelCode>{`=PIVOTBY(C2:C76, A2:A76, D2:D76, PERCENTOF, , , , , , , 2) → each cell as a % of the grand total`}</ExcelCode>

`relative_to` switches between column totals (the default), row
totals, the grand total, or a parent row/column in a multi-level
grouping — four distinct "percent of what, exactly" questions a
single static PivotTable view can only answer one of at a time.

<Callout type="warn">
  All three divide by a sum. If that sum is zero — every value in
  `data_all` is zero, blank, or the range is genuinely empty —
  PERCENTOF, and any GROUPBY/PIVOTBY call built on it, returns
  `#DIV/0!` like any other division. Wrap in `IFERROR` if a zero
  total is a real possibility, not just a sign something upstream is
  broken.
</Callout>

## When a PivotTable still wins

Nothing here replaces a PivotTable for genuinely interactive
exploration — dragging fields around, slicers, drilling into a
cell's source rows with a double-click. What changes is the narrower
case: a report that always needs the same "% of total by category"
breakdown, recalculating live as source data changes, directly
composable with other formulas around it. That's the case a
PivotTable was always a slightly heavy tool for, and it's now a
one-line formula instead.
