Evaluate the modified Bessel function of the second kind, order `N`,
at `X` — equivalent to [BESSELY](/docs/engineering/bessely) evaluated
for a purely imaginary argument, and the natural pair to
[BESSELI](/docs/engineering/besseli) the same way BESSELY pairs with
[BESSELJ](/docs/engineering/besselj).

## Return value

The value of the order-`N` modified Bessel function of the second
kind, evaluated at `X`.

## Description

BESSELK is [BESSELI](/docs/engineering/besseli)'s independent
counterpart — together they form the two solutions to the modified
Bessel differential equation, the same way
[BESSELJ](/docs/engineering/besselj) and
[BESSELY](/docs/engineering/bessely) form the two solutions to the
ordinary Bessel equation. Where BESSELI grows exponentially larger as
`X` increases, BESSELK does the opposite — it decays exponentially
toward `0`, making the pair a natural fit for physical problems with
one solution that grows and one that decays (steady-state heat
conduction outward from a cylindrical source, for instance).

<Callout type="warn">
  BESSELK is undefined at `X = 0` (it diverges to infinity there) and
  for any negative `X` — unlike
  [BESSELI](/docs/engineering/besseli) and
  [BESSELJ](/docs/engineering/besselj), which both accept zero or
  negative `X` without complaint.
</Callout>

At a fixed `X` of `1.9`, BESSELK's values across the first few orders
are all fairly close together and small — a much gentler spread than
[BESSELI](/docs/engineering/besseli)'s at the same `X`, since BESSELK
is the decaying (not growing) half of the modified pair.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — X is zero or negative

<ExcelCode>{`BESSELK(0, 1) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Unlike BESSELI and BESSELJ, BESSELK requires `X` to be strictly
greater than `0` — it's mathematically undefined (diverging to
infinity) at `X = 0`, and undefined entirely for negative `X`.

### #NUM! — N is negative

<ExcelCode>{`BESSELK(1.9, -1) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

The order `N` must be `0` or greater. A negative order returns
`#NUM!`.

### #VALUE! — X or N is nonnumeric

<ExcelCode>{`BESSELK("x", 2) → #VALUE!`}</ExcelCode>

Both arguments must be actual numbers — text that can't be interpreted
as one returns `#VALUE!`.
