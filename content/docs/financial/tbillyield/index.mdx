Calculate a Treasury bill's **yield** from its price — the reverse
direction of [TBILLPRICE](/docs/financial/tbillprice), turning a
known purchase price back into the investment return rate it
implies.

## Return value

The yield implied by `pr` — equal to `(100-pr)/pr * (360/DSM)`,
where DSM is the number of days between `settlement` and `maturity`.

## Description

TBILLYIELD answers the question TBILLPRICE doesn't: given what was
actually paid for a T-bill, what return does that price represent?
It's the natural companion function — TBILLPRICE goes from a quoted
discount rate to a price, TBILLYIELD goes from a price back to the
investment yield that price implies (a subtly different number from
the original discount rate, since yield is measured against the
*price paid*, not the face value).

<ExcelCode>{`=TBILLYIELD("3/31/2008", "6/1/2008", 98.45) → 0.0914, or 9.14%`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="idea">
  **TBILLYIELD's result isn't the same number as the discount rate
  that produced the price.** Discount rate is calculated against
  face value ($100); yield is calculated against the actual price
  paid (less than $100) — since the price paid is smaller than face
  value, the yield is always slightly *higher* than the discount
  rate that produced it.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — a non-positive price

<ExcelCode>{`TBILLYIELD("3/31/2008", "6/1/2008", 0) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`pr` must be strictly greater than `0`.

### Confusing TBILLYIELD's result with the original discount rate

<ExcelCode>{`A 9% discount rate produces a price that yields slightly more than 9% when run back through TBILLYIELD`}</ExcelCode>

The two numbers are related but genuinely different — discount rate
is against face value, yield is against the price actually paid.

        dsm = (maturity - settlement).days
        return (100 - pr) / pr * (360 / dsm)
