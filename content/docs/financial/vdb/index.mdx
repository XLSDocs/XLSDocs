Calculate declining-balance depreciation for **any specified period
range**, including a fractional/partial period — the most flexible
of this site's declining-balance depreciation functions, and the
only one that can report depreciation summed across multiple
periods in a single call.

## Return value

The total depreciation across the span from `start_period` to
`end_period`, using declining-balance at `factor` (double-declining
by default), switching to straight-line partway through unless
`no_switch` is `TRUE`.

## Description

VDB generalizes [DDB](/docs/financial/ddb) in two ways: it can
report depreciation summed across a *range* of periods (not just
one), including a genuinely fractional start or end point for a
partial period, and — unless `no_switch` is set — it automatically
switches from declining-balance to straight-line partway through the
asset's life, at exactly the point where straight-line would produce
*more* depreciation than declining-balance would from there on. This
switch matters because pure declining-balance alone never fully
depreciates an asset down to its salvage value on its own.

<ExcelCode>{`=VDB(10000, 1000, 5, 0, 1) → 4000 (year 1 of a 5-year double-declining-balance schedule — matches DDB's own first-year result)`}</ExcelCode>

<ExcelCode>{`=VDB(10000, 1000, 5, 0, 5) → 9000 (summed across the full 5-year life, exactly cost - salvage, thanks to the automatic switch to straight-line)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="idea">
  **The automatic switch to straight-line is what fully depreciates
  the asset by the end of its life.** Pure declining-balance,
  unmodified, approaches `salvage` but never actually reaches it —
  VDB's default behavior (unless `no_switch=TRUE`) crosses over to
  straight-line exactly when that produces the larger depreciation
  amount, so the total across the full `life` lands exactly on
  `cost - salvage`.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — start_period or end_period outside the asset's life

<ExcelCode>{`VDB(10000, 1000, 5, 0, 6) → #NUM! (end_period of 6 exceeds the 5-period life)`}</ExcelCode>

`start_period` and `end_period` both need to fall within `0` to
`life`.

### Forgetting start_period/end_period use life's units, not dates

<ExcelCode>{`VDB(10000, 1000, 5, "1/1/2024", "12/31/2024") → #VALUE! — these are period numbers, not calendar dates`}</ExcelCode>

Unlike the French-accounting depreciation functions
([AMORDEGRC](/docs/financial/amordegrc)/[AMORLINC](/docs/financial/amorlinc)),
VDB's periods are plain numbers in whatever unit `life` is expressed
in (years, months, etc.) — not calendar dates.
