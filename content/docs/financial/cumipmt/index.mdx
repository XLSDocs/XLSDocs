Calculate the total interest paid across a whole range of payment
periods in one call — the reliable alternative to summing IPMT for
every individual period by hand.

## Return value

The total interest paid from `start_period` through `end_period`
(inclusive), as a negative number — the sum of `IPMT` across that
same range, computed directly instead of added up manually.

## Description

CUMIPMT saves building a manual `SUM` of `IPMT` calls across a range
of periods — a common but easy-to-get-wrong pattern, since it
requires the range to exactly match `nper` or the intended sub-range.
A frequent real use: finding the total interest paid in a single
calendar year (12 consecutive periods) partway through a loan, or the
total interest over the loan's entire life (`start_period=1`,
`end_period=nper`).

<Callout type="warn">
  Unlike `PMT`/`IPMT`/`PPMT`, CUMIPMT's `type` argument is
  **required**, not optional — it must always be supplied (`0` or
  `1`), even though the equivalent argument is optional everywhere
  else in Excel's amortization functions.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — start_period or end_period is out of range

<ExcelCode>{`CUMIPMT(5%/12, 360, 200000, 0, 12, 0) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Both period arguments must fall between `1` and `nper`, and
`start_period` can't exceed `end_period` — period `0` doesn't exist.

### #NUM! — rate, nper, or pv isn't positive

<ExcelCode>{`CUMIPMT(5%/12, 360, -200000, 1, 12, 0) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Unlike `PMT`, CUMIPMT requires `rate`, `nper`, and `pv` to all be
positive numbers — it doesn't use the same negative-for-outflow sign
convention on this particular argument.

### Forgetting the required type argument

<ExcelCode>{`CUMIPMT(5%/12, 360, 200000, 1, 12) → #VALUE! (missing argument)`}</ExcelCode>

`type` is required for CUMIPMT specifically, unlike PMT/IPMT/PPMT
where the equivalent argument can be omitted and defaults to `0`.
