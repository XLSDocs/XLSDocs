Calculate the price of a bond whose **first coupon period doesn't
match the regular schedule** — shorter or longer than every coupon
period after it, which happens whenever a bond's issue date doesn't
line up neatly with its normal coupon spacing.

## Return value

The price per $100 face value of a bond whose first coupon period
is shorter or longer than every regular period that follows it.

## Description

Most bonds are issued right at the start of a coupon period, so
[PRICE](/docs/financial/price) can assume every period — including
the first — is the same length. ODDFPRICE handles the case where
that's not true: `issue` falls somewhere that makes the stretch from
`issue` to `first_coupon` either shorter or longer than a normal
`12/frequency`-month period.

<Callout type="warn">
  **ODDFPRICE uses one of two genuinely different formulas
  internally**, depending on whether the odd first period is
  shorter or longer than a regular one. The short case prorates a
  single partial coupon; the long case splits the odd period into
  several normal-length "quasi-coupon" periods and sums the present
  value of each slice separately. You don't choose which — Excel
  detects it automatically from your dates.
</Callout>

<ExcelCode>{`=ODDFPRICE(DATE(2008,11,11), DATE(2021,3,1), DATE(2008,10,15), DATE(2009,3,1), 7.85%, 6.25%, 100, 2, 1) → $113.60 (Microsoft's own documented example — a short first period)`}</ExcelCode>

Just like a regular bond, a higher `yld` prices this one lower — the
odd first period changes exactly how the discounting is done, not
the basic inverse relationship between yield and price.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — dates out of the required order

<ExcelCode>{`ODDFPRICE(DATE(2009,3,1), DATE(2021,3,1), DATE(2008,10,15), DATE(2008,11,11), 7.85%, 6.25%, 100, 2) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Excel requires `maturity > first_coupon > settlement > issue` —
all four dates have to fall in exactly that order.

### #NUM! — a negative rate or yield

<ExcelCode>{`ODDFPRICE(DATE(2008,11,11), DATE(2021,3,1), DATE(2008,10,15), DATE(2009,3,1), -7.85%, 6.25%, 100, 2) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Both `rate` and `yld` have to be zero or positive.

    ```python lineNumbers
    # Covers only the "short odd first period" case (issue closer to
    # first_coupon than a full regular period) — the "long" case needs
    # a separate quasi-coupon-period summation entirely
    def oddfprice_short(settlement, maturity, issue, first_coupon, rate, yld,
                         redemption, frequency, e, n):
        # e: days in the regular period ending at first_coupon
        # n: number of coupons payable between settlement and maturity
        dfc = (first_coupon - issue).days
        dsc = (first_coupon - settlement).days
        a = (settlement - issue).days
        x = 1 + yld / frequency
        y = dsc / e
        coupon = 100 * rate / frequency
        price = redemption / x ** (n - 1 + y)
        price += coupon * dfc / e / x ** y
        for k in range(2, n + 1):
            price += coupon / x ** (k - 1 + y)
        price -= a / e * coupon
        return price
    ```
