Calculate the **price per $100 face value** of a Treasury bill,
given its discount rate — T-bills are sold at a discount to face
value rather than paying periodic interest, so the "price" itself is
the calculation of interest here.

## Return value

The price per $100 of face value — equal to `100 * (1 - discount * DSM/360)`, where DSM is the
number of days between `settlement` and `maturity`.

## Description

Unlike a bond, a Treasury bill pays no periodic interest — it's sold
below its face value and matures at full face value, with the
discount *being* the investor's return. TBILLPRICE converts the
quoted discount rate into the actual price an investor pays per $100
of face value.

<ExcelCode>{`=TBILLPRICE("3/31/2008", "6/1/2008", 0.09) → 98.45 (a 62-day bill at a 9% discount rate)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="idea">
  TBILLPRICE uses a **360-day year** for the day count, the standard
  money-market convention for Treasury bills — not the 365-day year
  ordinary calendar math would suggest.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — a non-positive discount rate

<ExcelCode>{`TBILLPRICE("3/31/2008", "6/1/2008", 0) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`discount` must be strictly greater than `0`.

### #NUM! — maturity more than a year past settlement

<ExcelCode>{`TBILLPRICE("1/1/2024", "6/1/2025", 0.05) → #NUM! (more than 1 year between the dates)`}</ExcelCode>

Treasury bills mature within a year of issue by definition — a
longer span isn't a valid T-bill and returns `#NUM!`.

    from datetime import date

    def tbillprice(settlement, maturity, discount):
        dsm = (maturity - settlement).days
        return 100 * (1 - discount * dsm / 360)
