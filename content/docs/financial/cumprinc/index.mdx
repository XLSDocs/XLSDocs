Calculate the total principal paid down across a whole range of
payment periods in one call — the reliable alternative to summing
PPMT for every individual period by hand.

## Return value

The total principal paid down from `start_period` through
`end_period` (inclusive), as a negative number — the sum of `PPMT`
across that same range.

## Description

CUMPRINC is CUMIPMT's counterpart: instead of totaling the interest
portion of a range of payments, it totals the principal portion — how
much of the loan balance actually got paid down over that stretch.
Summed across the entire loan (`start_period=1`, `end_period=nper`),
CUMPRINC always comes out to exactly `-pv` — every dollar borrowed
eventually gets paid back as principal, by definition.

<Callout type="idea">
  A useful sanity check for any amortization model: `CUMPRINC(rate,
  nper, pv, 1, nper, type)` should always equal `-pv` exactly. If it
  doesn't, something in the loan's other assumptions (rate, nper, or
  a mismatched `fv`) is inconsistent.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — start_period or end_period is out of range

<ExcelCode>{`CUMPRINC(5%/12, 360, 200000, 0, 12, 0) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Both period arguments must fall between `1` and `nper`, and
`start_period` can't exceed `end_period`.

### The full-loan total doesn't equal -pv exactly

If `CUMPRINC(rate, nper, pv, 1, nper, type)` doesn't come out to
`-pv`, double-check that `rate`, `nper`, and `pv` are all consistent
with each other (and that `fv` — implicitly 0 here — actually matches
the loan being fully paid off by the end).

### Forgetting the required type argument

<ExcelCode>{`CUMPRINC(5%/12, 360, 200000, 1, 12) → #VALUE! (missing argument)`}</ExcelCode>

`type` is required for CUMPRINC specifically, unlike PMT/IPMT/PPMT
where the equivalent argument can be omitted and defaults to `0`.
