Calculate depreciation using the sum-of-years'-digits method — a
moderately accelerated schedule, front-loaded like DB and DDB but
gentler, that always fully depreciates the asset to its salvage value
by the end of its useful life.

## Return value

The depreciation expense for `per`: `(cost − salvage) × (life − per +
1) / (sum of 1 through life)`.

## Description

SYD weights each period by its position in a countdown — the "sum of
years' digits" for a 10-year life is `1+2+...+10 = 55`, and period 1
gets weighted `10/55` of the total depreciable amount, period 2 gets
`9/55`, and so on down to period 10's `1/55`. This produces a
front-loaded schedule, but one that decreases in even steps rather
than DB/DDB's compounding percentage decline — and unlike DDB, the
weights are guaranteed to sum to exactly 1, so the full depreciable
amount (`cost − salvage`) is always fully allocated by the last
period.

<Callout type="idea">
  SYD is the one accelerated method here that **always** lands
  exactly on `salvage` after the final period, with no rounding
  shortfall the way DB can have and no floor-related shortfall the
  way DDB often has. If a schedule needs to fully deplete to salvage
  precisely, SYD is the more predictable choice of the three
  accelerated methods.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — per is greater than life, or life is 0

<ExcelCode>{`SYD(50000, 5000, 10, 11) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`per` can't exceed `life` — there's no 11th period in a 10-period
schedule.

### Expecting the same front-loading intensity as DDB

<ExcelCode>{`SYD(50000, 5000, 10, 1) → $8,181.82 vs. DDB(50000, 5000, 10, 1) → $10,000.00`}</ExcelCode>

SYD is a gentler accelerated method than DDB — if a more aggressive
early write-off is actually needed, DDB or DB produce a steeper
front-loaded schedule.
