Calculate the **amount paid out at maturity** for a security bought
at a discount — the inverse of INTRATE: given how much was invested
and the discount rate, find the redemption amount.

## Return value

The total amount received at maturity for a fully invested, no-coupon
security — always larger than `investment`, scaled up by `discount`
annualized over the span from `settlement` to `maturity`.

## Description

RECEIVED answers the question INTRATE's inputs ask in reverse: given
an amount invested and a known discount rate, how much comes back at
maturity?

<ExcelCode>{`RECEIVED = investment / (1 - discount × (days between settlement and maturity) / (days in a year, per basis))`}</ExcelCode>

<ExcelCode>{`=RECEIVED(DATE(2025,1,1), DATE(2025,7,1), 1000, 5%, 0) → $1,025.64 (a $1,000 investment at a 5% discount rate, basis 0, over exactly half a year)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="idea">
  **RECEIVED is INTRATE solved the other way around.** INTRATE takes
  a known investment and redemption amount and solves for the rate;
  RECEIVED takes a known investment and rate and solves for the
  redemption amount. Plugging RECEIVED's own output back into
  INTRATE (alongside the same investment, dates, and basis) returns
  the original `discount` rate.
</Callout>

A higher `discount` rate scales up the amount received at maturity
by more — the gap between the fixed `$1,000` investment and the
payout widens as the discount rate climbs.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — settlement on or after maturity

<ExcelCode>{`RECEIVED(DATE(2025,7,1), DATE(2025,1,1), 1000, 5%) → #NUM! (settlement is after maturity)`}</ExcelCode>

`settlement` has to fall strictly before `maturity`.

### #NUM! — a discount rate at or above 100% of the period

<ExcelCode>{`RECEIVED(DATE(2025,1,1), DATE(2025,7,1), 1000, 250%, 0) → #NUM! (discount × days/year ≥ 1, dividing by zero or less)`}</ExcelCode>

If `discount` multiplied by the fraction of a year between
`settlement` and `maturity` reaches or exceeds `1`, RECEIVED's
denominator hits zero or goes negative — an economically meaningless
discount rate for the period, not just a large one.

  <CodeBlockTab value="Python">
    ```python lineNumbers
    from datetime import date

    def received(settlement, maturity, investment, discount, year_days=360):
        # basis=0 (NASD 30/360) assumed here for the day span too — a
        # real 30/360 day count differs from calendar subtraction once
        # a month doesn't have exactly 30 days
        dim = (maturity - settlement).days
        return investment / (1 - discount * dim / year_days)

    payout = received(date(2025, 1, 1), date(2025, 7, 1), 1000, 0.05)
    print(payout)
    ```
