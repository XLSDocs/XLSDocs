Calculate depreciation for an accounting period under the **French
accounting system**, using a flat straight-line rate — the simpler
counterpart to [AMORDEGRC](/docs/financial/amordegrc)'s accelerated,
life-coefficient-based method.

## Return value

The depreciation amount for `period`. A full period's depreciation
is a flat `cost * rate`; the first (partial) period is prorated by
the actual number of days between `date_purchased` and
`first_period`.

## Description

AMORLINC is the French accounting system's straight-line
depreciation method — every full accounting period depreciates the
asset by the same flat amount (`cost * rate`), with the one
exception being the very first period, which is shortened to
reflect however much of that period the asset was actually owned
before `first_period`'s cutoff.

<ExcelCode>{`=AMORLINC(2400, "8/19/2008", "12/31/2008", 300, 1, 0.15, 1) → 360 (a full period's flat depreciation, equal to cost * rate)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="idea">
  **AMORLINC's period 1 result here is exactly `cost * rate`
  (`2400 * 0.15 = 360`), with no proration** — the proration happens
  in the implicit stub period *before* period 1, covering the 134
  actual days between the August purchase date and the December
  `first_period` cutoff. From period 1 onward, each full period
  depreciates by the same flat amount, unlike
  [AMORDEGRC](/docs/financial/amordegrc)'s accelerating coefficient.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — salvage value greater than cost

<ExcelCode>{`AMORLINC(2400, "8/19/2008", "12/31/2008", 3000, 1, 0.15) → #NUM! (salvage can't exceed cost)`}</ExcelCode>

An asset can't have a salvage value worth more than what it
originally cost.

### #NUM! — a purchase date after the first period ends

<ExcelCode>{`AMORLINC(2400, "1/1/2009", "12/31/2008", 300, 1, 0.15) → #NUM! (date_purchased is after first_period)`}</ExcelCode>

`date_purchased` has to fall on or before `first_period`'s cutoff
date — otherwise there's no valid span to prorate the first
period's depreciation across.
