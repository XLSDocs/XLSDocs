Convert an effective annual interest rate back into the nominal
(stated) rate — the exact reverse of `EFFECT`, given how often the
rate compounds per year.

## Return value

The nominal (stated) annual interest rate: `npery × ((1 +
effect_rate)^(1/npery) − 1)`.

## Description

NOMINAL answers the reverse question from EFFECT: given a real,
effective annual rate that's actually being earned or charged, what
stated rate would produce that result once a given compounding
schedule is applied? This is the calculation behind converting a
target effective return into the nominal rate to quote or search for
on a specific product.

<Callout type="idea">
  `NOMINAL` and `EFFECT` are exact inverses of each other:
  `NOMINAL(EFFECT(rate, npery), npery)` always returns `rate` again,
  round-trip. For a fixed effective rate, a **higher** `npery`
  produces a **lower** required nominal rate — the opposite direction
  from EFFECT, where more compounding periods raise the effective
  rate for a fixed nominal one.
</Callout>

To achieve the same 6% effective annual rate, less frequent
compounding needs a *higher* stated nominal rate: annual compounding
needs exactly 6% nominal, but monthly compounding only needs about
5.84% nominal to reach that same 6% effective result.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — effect_rate or npery isn't positive

<ExcelCode>{`NOMINAL(-0.06, 12) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Both arguments must be greater than 0 — a negative or zero rate or
compounding frequency isn't valid.

### Confusing which rate is which

<ExcelCode>{`NOMINAL(0.06, 12) → the nominal rate that PRODUCES a 6% effective rate, not 6% itself`}</ExcelCode>

The first argument is the *effective* rate going in — the result is
the (different, typically lower) nominal rate needed to reach it.
Mixing up which rate is the input is the most common NOMINAL mistake.
