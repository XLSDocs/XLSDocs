Convert a nominal (stated) annual interest rate into the effective
annual rate — the real rate actually earned or paid once compounding
within the year is accounted for.

## Return value

The effective annual interest rate: `(1 + nominal_rate/npery)^npery −
1`.

## Description

A "6% APR" advertised rate doesn't actually mean 6% is earned or
charged over a year — if that 6% compounds monthly, the real
(effective) annual rate is slightly higher, since each month's
interest itself starts earning interest too. EFFECT converts the
stated nominal rate into that real, comparable figure, which is the
only fair way to compare two rates that compound on different
schedules (say, monthly vs. quarterly).

<Callout type="idea">
  The more frequently a rate compounds, the larger the gap between
  nominal and effective becomes — daily compounding pushes the
  effective rate higher than monthly compounding does, for the same
  stated nominal rate. With `npery = 1` (compounding once a year),
  EFFECT simply returns `nominal_rate` unchanged, since there's no
  intra-year compounding to account for.
</Callout>

The same 6% nominal rate produces a higher effective rate the more
often it compounds — annual compounding (`npery=1`) leaves it
unchanged at exactly 6%, while daily compounding (`npery=365`) pushes
it up to roughly 6.18%.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — nominal_rate or npery isn't positive

<ExcelCode>{`EFFECT(-0.06, 12) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Both arguments must be greater than 0 — a negative or zero rate or
compounding frequency isn't valid.

### Comparing two rates without converting both to effective first

<ExcelCode>{`6% compounded monthly vs. 6.1% compounded annually → not directly comparable as stated`}</ExcelCode>

Two nominal rates with different compounding frequencies can't be
fairly compared as-is — convert both with EFFECT first, then compare
the resulting effective rates.
