Calculate what a series of future payments is worth in today's
money — the lump sum that, invested now at a given rate, would fund
that exact stream of payments.

## Return value

The present-day value of the future payment stream. Following the
cash-flow sign convention, the result's sign is opposite `pmt` — a
series of payments you'll make (negative `pmt`) has a positive
present value (money you'd need to receive now to fund them).

## Description

PV answers "what's this stream of future payments worth today,"
given a discount rate — the same math that determines how much a
lender is willing to give you now in exchange for a promised series
of loan payments, or what a pension/annuity payout stream is worth
as a lump sum today.

<Callout type="warn">
  Like PMT and FV, PV follows the cash-flow sign convention:
  `pmt` (money you pay out each period) is negative, and the
  resulting `PV` comes back **positive** — the lump sum you'd need to
  receive now to exactly fund those payments. This is the reverse of
  what many people expect from a "present value of my future costs"
  question.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Result sign looks backwards

<ExcelCode>{`PV(5%/12, 30*12, 1073.64) → negative (pmt entered as positive)`}</ExcelCode>

`pmt` should be negative for a payment made — flip the sign to get a
positive present value:

<ExcelCode>{`PV(5%/12, 30*12, -1073.64)`}</ExcelCode>

### Result is far too large or small

<ExcelCode>{`PV(5%, 30*12, -1073.64) → wildly wrong (annual rate used against a monthly nper)`}</ExcelCode>

`rate` and `nper` must match periods — divide the annual rate to get
a monthly one:

<ExcelCode>{`PV(5%/12, 30*12, -1073.64)`}</ExcelCode>
