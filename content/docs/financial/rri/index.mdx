Calculate the **equivalent constant interest rate** that explains
how `pv` grew into `fv` over `nper` periods — the standard way to
compute a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) from a start value, an
end value, and a time span.

## Return value

The constant per-period rate that would grow `pv` into `fv` over
`nper` periods — equal to `(fv/pv)^(1/nper) - 1`.

## Description

RRI answers "what single constant rate explains this growth,"
working backward from two known values and a time span — exactly
the Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) calculation analysts use to
summarize an investment's performance as one comparable number,
regardless of how bumpy its actual year-to-year returns were.

<ExcelCode>{`=RRI(8, 10000, 11000) → 0.012 (an investment of $10,000 growing to $11,000 over 8 years implies a 1.2% annual rate)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="warn">
  **RRI assumes a single lump sum growing on its own** — no
  recurring contributions or withdrawals along the way. If the
  investment also involves regular deposits or payments, use
  [RATE](/docs/financial/rate) instead, which accounts for a
  recurring `pmt` on top of the lump-sum growth.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Using RRI when deposits/withdrawals were also happening

<ExcelCode>{`RRI assumes pv alone grew into fv — if money was added or withdrawn along the way, the result is meaningless`}</ExcelCode>

Switch to [RATE](/docs/financial/rate), which has a `pmt` argument
for exactly this case.

### #NUM! — mismatched signs or invalid inputs

<ExcelCode>{`RRI(8, 0, 11000) → #NUM! (pv can't be 0)`}</ExcelCode>

`pv` can't be `0` (there's no rate that grows nothing into
something), and other invalid combinations return `#NUM!` as well.
