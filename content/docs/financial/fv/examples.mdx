---
title: FV examples
description: Worked examples for FV — regular contributions, an existing lump sum, and the sign-convention gotcha.
---

<FunctionNav />

<ExampleCard number="01" label="Monthly contribution over 10 years" formula={`=FV(5%/12, 10*12, -200)`}>
  Contributing $200/month at 5% annual interest for 10 years —
  `pmt` is negative since it's money being paid in.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="02" label="Starting from an existing balance" formula={`=FV(5%/12, 10*12, -200, -5000)`}>
  Adding an existing $5,000 balance (`pv`, also negative — money
  already committed) on top of the monthly contributions.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="03" label="Lump sum only, no ongoing contributions" formula={`=FV(6%/12, 20*12, 0, -10000)`}>
  A one-time $10,000 investment left to grow for 20 years, with no
  further contributions (`pmt` is 0).
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="04" label="Contributions at the start of each period" formula={`=FV(5%/12, 10*12, -200, 0, 1)`}>
  Setting `type` to `1` produces a slightly higher future value,
  since each contribution has one extra period to accrue interest.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="05" label="Positive pmt gives the wrong sign" formula={`=FV(5%/12, 10*12, 200)`}>
  Returns a negative number — `pmt` was entered as positive
  (backwards from the convention), producing a future value that
  looks like a debt instead of savings.
</ExampleCard>
