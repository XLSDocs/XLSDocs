Calculate what an investment will be worth after a series of equal
payments at a constant interest rate — how much a regular
contribution grows into by a target date.

## Return value

The value of the investment after `nper` periods. Following the same
sign convention as PMT/PV, the result is positive when `pmt`/`pv` are
negative (money you contributed) — it represents money you'd receive.

## Description

FV is the mirror image of PV — instead of discounting future
payments back to today's value, it projects a series of equal
contributions (or a lump sum, or both) forward to a future value.
It's the formula behind "how much will my monthly contribution be
worth in 20 years" calculators.

<Callout type="warn">
  `pmt` follows the same sign convention as PMT's `pv` — a regular
  contribution you make is money leaving your pocket, so it's entered
  as **negative**. `FV(5%/12, 10*12, -200)` (contributing $200/month)
  returns a **positive** number — the value you'll have, money you'll
  effectively receive back later.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Result is negative when a positive future value was expected

<ExcelCode>{`FV(5%/12, 10*12, 200) → negative (pmt entered as positive, the opposite of the convention)`}</ExcelCode>

`pmt` should be negative for a contribution — flip its sign:

<ExcelCode>{`FV(5%/12, 10*12, -200)`}</ExcelCode>

### Result is far too large or small

<ExcelCode>{`FV(5%, 10*12, -200) → wildly wrong (5% used as a monthly rate on a term already converted to months)`}</ExcelCode>

`rate` and `nper` must be in the same period — a monthly contribution
schedule needs a monthly rate:

<ExcelCode>{`FV(5%/12, 10*12, -200)`}</ExcelCode>
