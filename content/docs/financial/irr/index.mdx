Find the discount rate that makes a series of cash flows break
even — the rate at which their net present value equals exactly
zero. The standard way to compare investments or projects with
different cash flow timing on a single, comparable number.

## Return value

The discount rate that makes the net present value of `values`
equal to zero. Returns `#NUM!` if the underlying iterative solver
can't converge, or if `values` contains no sign change at all.

## Description

IRR answers the same underlying question as NPV, in reverse: instead
of computing the present value at a known rate, it finds the rate
that makes the present value exactly zero. Unlike RATE (which
assumes equal payments every period), IRR handles cash flows that
genuinely vary period to period — the same shape NPV expects.

<Callout type="warn">
  `values` must include the **initial investment as its first
  entry**, typically negative (money paid out), followed by the
  return cash flows. Unlike NPV, where the initial investment is
  added *outside* the function call, IRR expects it included
  directly *inside* `values` — the opposite convention.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — no sign change in values

<ExcelCode>{`IRR({1000, 2000, 3000}) → #NUM! (all positive, no investment to recover)`}</ExcelCode>

`values` needs at least one negative value (the investment) and at
least one positive value (the return) — a rate of return is
undefined without both.

### #NUM! — the solver failed to converge

Some cash flow patterns converge slowly or not at all from the
default 10% starting guess. Supplying a `guess` closer to the
expected real answer often resolves it:

<ExcelCode>{`IRR({-50000, 15000, 18000, 20000}, 15%)`}</ExcelCode>

### Forgetting the initial investment inside values

<ExcelCode>{`IRR({15000, 18000, 20000}) → wrong (missing the initial outflow entirely)`}</ExcelCode>

Unlike NPV, IRR needs the initial investment as part of `values`
itself, not added separately:

<ExcelCode>{`IRR({-50000, 15000, 18000, 20000})`}</ExcelCode>

## Related functions

- [NPV](/docs/financial/npv) — the present value at a known rate,
  instead of solving for the rate itself.
- [XIRR](/docs/financial/xirr) — the same idea, but for cash flows
  that land on irregular, specific dates instead of even periods.
- [RATE](/docs/financial/rate) — solves for a rate too, but assumes
  equal payments every period rather than IRR's varying cash flows.
- [PMT](/docs/financial/pmt) and [PV](/docs/financial/pv) — assume
  a constant payment amount, a simpler shape than IRR handles.
