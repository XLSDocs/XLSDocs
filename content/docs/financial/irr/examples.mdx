---
title: IRR examples
description: Worked examples for IRR — a basic project return, supplying a guess, and comparing against NPV.
---

<FunctionNav />

<ExampleCard number="01" label="Basic — rate of return on a project" formula={`=IRR({-50000, 15000, 18000, 20000})`}>
  A $50,000 initial investment followed by three years of returns —
  IRR finds the rate at which this breaks even.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="02" label="Using a range instead of literal values" formula={`=IRR(Cashflows[Amount])`}>
  IRR accepts a range directly — cleaner than listing each cash flow
  as a separate value once there are more than a few.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="03" label="Supplying a guess for a hard-to-converge case" formula={`=IRR(Cashflows[Amount], 20%)`}>
  A starting `guess` closer to the expected real rate helps the
  iterative solver converge on genuinely difficult cash flow
  patterns.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="04" label="Checking against a required return" formula={`=IRR(Cashflows[Amount]) > 12%`}>
  Compares the calculated IRR against a required rate of return —
  TRUE means the project clears the bar.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="05" label="Verifying with NPV at the IRR rate" formula={`=NPV(IRR({-50000,15000,18000,20000}), {15000,18000,20000}) - 50000`}>
  Feeding IRR's own result back into NPV (excluding the initial
  investment from the NPV call, same as any other NPV usage) should
  land at very close to zero — a useful sanity check on the
  calculation.
</ExampleCard>
