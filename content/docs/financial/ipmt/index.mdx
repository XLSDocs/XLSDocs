Calculate how much of one specific payment goes toward interest, as
opposed to reducing the loan balance — the other half of PPMT's
principal/interest breakdown.

## Return value

The interest portion of the payment identified by `per`, as a
negative number (money paid out), following the same cash-flow sign
convention as PMT.

## Description

IPMT calculates interest on whatever balance remains at the start of
period `per` — since a standard loan's balance shrinks over time, the
interest charge (and therefore IPMT's result) shrinks with it. The
first payment carries the most interest of any payment in the loan;
the last carries the least.

<Callout type="info">
  For any payment period, `IPMT(...) + PPMT(...)` (with identical
  arguments) always equals the total payment from `PMT(...)` — these
  two functions split the same fixed payment, they don't calculate
  independent amounts.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — per is outside the valid range

<ExcelCode>{`IPMT(5%/12, 0, 360, 200000) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`per` must be between `1` and `nper`. Payment "0" doesn't exist —
the first payment is period 1.

### The interest amount seems too high on early payments

This is expected, not an error. Interest is charged on the full
remaining balance, which is highest right after the loan starts —
IPMT's result is largest on the first payment and decreases every
period after that as the balance is paid down.

### Total interest paid over the life of the loan is wrong

Summing IPMT across every period (`=SUM(IPMT(rate, 1, nper, pv), ...,
IPMT(rate, nper, nper, pv))`) gives total interest paid — but it's
easy to get this wrong by summing a fixed number of rows that doesn't
actually match `nper`. Use `CUMIPMT` instead when the total across a
whole range of periods is needed, rather than building the sum by
hand.
