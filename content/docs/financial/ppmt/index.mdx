Calculate how much of one specific payment goes toward paying down
the principal (the original loan balance), as opposed to interest —
the building block behind a full amortization schedule.

## Return value

The principal portion of the payment identified by `per`, as a
negative number (money paid out), following the same cash-flow sign
convention as PMT.

## Description

Every loan payment splits into two parts: interest on the remaining
balance, and principal that actually reduces the balance. PPMT
isolates the principal part of one specific payment — early payments
on a standard loan are mostly interest, and the principal share grows
with each payment as the balance shrinks.

<Callout type="info">
  For any payment period, `PPMT(...) + IPMT(...)` (with identical
  arguments) always equals the total payment from `PMT(...)` — the
  two functions are a full breakdown of the same fixed payment, not
  independent calculations.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — per is outside the valid range

<ExcelCode>{`PPMT(5%/12, 0, 360, 200000) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`per` must be between `1` and `nper`. Payment "0" doesn't exist —
the first payment is period 1.

### The principal amount looks backwards — too small early, too large late

This is expected, not an error. Early payments on a standard
amortizing loan are mostly interest (the balance is still high), so
the principal portion starts small and grows every period as the
balance shrinks — the exact opposite pattern from what intuition
suggests.

### Building a full schedule returns the wrong total

A full amortization schedule needs `per` to increment by 1 in each
row (`per=1`, `per=2`, `per=3`, ...), typically via a relative
reference like `=PPMT($B$1, ROW()-1, $B$2, $B$3)` dragged down a
column — reusing the same `per` value in every row is the most common
mistake when building one by hand.
