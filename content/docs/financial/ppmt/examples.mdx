title: PPMT examples
description: Worked examples for PPMT — the first and last payment of a loan, and building a full amortization schedule.
---

<FunctionNav />

<ExampleCard number="01" label="Principal portion of the first payment" formula={`=PPMT(5%/12, 1, 30*12, 200000)`}>
  On a $200,000 loan at 5% over 30 years, only about $240.31 of the
  first ~$1,073.64 payment goes toward principal — the rest is
  interest.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="02" label="Principal portion of the last payment" formula={`=PPMT(5%/12, 360, 30*12, 200000)`}>
  By the final payment (period 360), almost the entire payment is
  principal — the balance is nearly paid off, so there's very little
  left to charge interest on.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="03" label="Confirming PPMT + IPMT equals PMT" formula={`=PPMT(5%/12,1,360,200000) + IPMT(5%/12,1,360,200000)`}>
  Adding the principal and interest portions of the same payment
  returns exactly the total payment PMT would calculate on its own.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="04" label="Building one row of an amortization schedule" formula={`=PPMT($B$1/12, ROW()-1, $B$2*12, $B$3)`}>
  Using absolute references for the loan terms and `ROW()-1` for
  `per` lets this formula be dragged down a column to generate the
  principal portion of every payment.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="05" label="Invalid payment number" formula={`=PPMT(5%/12, 400, 360, 200000)`}>
  Returns `#NUM!` — period 400 doesn't exist on a 360-payment loan;
  `per` must be between 1 and `nper`.
</ExampleCard>
