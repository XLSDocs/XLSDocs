Calculate the interest accrued on a security that pays **all of its
interest in one lump sum at maturity** — no periodic coupons along
the way — rather than ACCRINT's periodic-coupon case.

## Return value

The total interest accrued between `issue` and `settlement` (the
maturity date), for a security that pays its entire interest amount
in one lump sum rather than periodically.

## Description

Most bonds pay interest on a schedule — semiannually, most commonly
— but some securities instead accumulate interest the whole time
they're outstanding and pay it all out in a single lump sum at
maturity. ACCRINTM calculates that accumulated amount as a simple
proportion of a full year's interest, scaled by how much of a year
(by day count) sits between `issue` and `settlement`:

<ExcelCode>{`ACCRINTM = par × rate × (days between issue and settlement) / (days in a year, per basis)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="warn">
  **This function's own `settlement` argument is actually the
  maturity date**, not a trade date like every other ACCRINT-style
  function's `settlement` argument. It's Microsoft's own naming
  choice, not a site convention — worth double-checking if you're
  used to ACCRINT's argument order.
</Callout>

Moving the maturity date further from the January 1 issue date
accrues proportionally more interest — a full year out, the result
lands exactly on a full year's coupon (`1000 × 5% = $50`), since
`basis 3` (actual/365) counts 365 actual days as one full year.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — issue is on or after settlement

<ExcelCode>{`ACCRINTM(DATE(2025,7,1), DATE(2025,1,1), 5%, 1000) → #NUM! (issue is after settlement)`}</ExcelCode>

`issue` has to fall strictly before `settlement` (the maturity
date) — there's no span to accrue interest over otherwise.

### Confusing ACCRINTM with ACCRINT

<ExcelCode>{`ACCRINTM(issue, settlement, rate, par) — only 4 required arguments, no frequency`}</ExcelCode>

ACCRINTM has no `frequency` argument at all, because it doesn't
need one — there's only ever one payment, at maturity.
[ACCRINT](/docs/financial/accrint) is the function for periodic
coupon bonds, and needs `frequency` to know how often those coupons
land.

    ```python lineNumbers
    from datetime import date

    def accrintm(issue, settlement, rate, par, year_days=365):
        # basis=3 (actual/365) only — other bases need a different
        # day-count convention for both the span and the year length
        days = (settlement - issue).days
        return par * rate * (days / year_days)

    accrued = accrintm(date(2025, 1, 1), date(2025, 7, 1), 0.05, 1000)
    print(accrued)
    ```
