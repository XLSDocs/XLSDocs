Calculate the yield of a bond whose **first coupon period doesn't
match the regular schedule** — the exact inverse of
[ODDFPRICE](/docs/financial/oddfprice).

## Return value

The annual yield implied by `pr`, for a bond whose first coupon
period is shorter or longer than every regular period that follows
it.

## Description

ODDFYIELD answers [ODDFPRICE](/docs/financial/oddfprice)'s question
in reverse: given a known price, what yield does that price imply?

<Callout type="warn">
  **Unlike [ODDLYIELD](/docs/financial/oddlyield), ODDFYIELD has no
  direct algebraic inverse** — the underlying ODDFPRICE formula
  isn't simple enough to rearrange and solve for `yld` directly.
  Excel finds it by iterative root-finding instead: trying
  candidate yields, running each one through the ODDFPRICE formula,
  and refining the guess until the resulting price matches `pr`.
  This is the same technique [YIELD](/docs/financial/yield) and
  [IRR](/docs/financial/irr) use internally.
</Callout>

<ExcelCode>{`=ODDFYIELD(DATE(2008,11,11), DATE(2021,3,1), DATE(2008,10,15), DATE(2009,3,1), 5.75%, 84.5, 100, 2, 0) → 7.72% (an 84.5 price implies this yield for a bond with a short first period)`}</ExcelCode>

A lower price implies a higher yield — the same inverse relationship
every other price/yield pair on this site follows, just computed
through ODDFPRICE's odd-first-period formula instead of the regular
one.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — dates out of the required order

<ExcelCode>{`ODDFYIELD(DATE(2009,3,1), DATE(2021,3,1), DATE(2008,10,15), DATE(2008,11,11), 5.75%, 84.5, 100, 2) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Excel requires `maturity > first_coupon > settlement > issue`.

### #NUM! — a negative rate or price

<ExcelCode>{`ODDFYIELD(DATE(2008,11,11), DATE(2021,3,1), DATE(2008,10,15), DATE(2009,3,1), 5.75%, -84.5, 100, 2) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

Both `rate` and `pr` have to be zero or positive.
