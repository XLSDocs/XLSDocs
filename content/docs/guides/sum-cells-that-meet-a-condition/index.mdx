---
title: How to sum cells that meet a condition
description: SUMIF for a single condition, SUMIFS for one or more — and the one argument-order difference between them that trips people up.
---

Summing values only for rows that match a condition — sales in one
region, invoices over a threshold, hours logged on a specific project
— is SUMIF and SUMIFS territory. The logic is simple; the one real
trap is that the two functions put their arguments in a different
order from each other.

## One condition: SUMIF

<ExcelCode>{`=SUMIF(B2:B100, "West", C2:C100)`}</ExcelCode>

[SUMIF](/docs/math/sumif) sums `C2:C100`, but only for rows where the
matching cell in `B2:B100` equals `"West"`. `sum_range` comes
**last**, and is even optional — leave it out and SUMIF sums the
condition range itself instead.

## Two or more conditions: SUMIFS

<ExcelCode>{`=SUMIFS(C2:C100, B2:B100, "West", D2:D100, ">500")`}</ExcelCode>

[SUMIFS](/docs/math/sumifs) sums `C2:C100` for rows where **every**
condition is true — region is "West" **and** amount in `D2:D100` is
over 500. Notice `sum_range` is now **first**.

<Callout type="warn">
  **SUMIF and SUMIFS put `sum_range` in opposite positions** — last in
  SUMIF, first in SUMIFS. Switching between the two functions (adding
  a second condition to an existing SUMIF, for instance) means
  reordering the arguments, not just appending a new pair at the end.
</Callout>

## Summing with a numeric comparison instead of an exact match

<ExcelCode>{`=SUMIF(C2:C100, ">500")`}</ExcelCode>

A condition like `">500"` works directly as text in the formula —
sums every value in `C2:C100` that's over 500, with no separate
condition range needed since the range being summed and the range
being tested are the same one here.

## Functions used in this guide
