Calculate what fraction of a full year lies between two dates — the
standard way to prorate an annual amount (a salary, a premium, a
subscription fee) across a partial year.

## Return value

The fraction of a year between `start_date` and `end_date`, as a
decimal — `0.5` for exactly half a year, `1.0` for exactly one full
year.

## Description

YEARFRAC answers "what portion of a year is this," which is exactly
what's needed to prorate anything billed or earned annually — an
employee who worked 6 months of the year earned `YEARFRAC(...)` of
their annual salary, roughly. The `basis` argument controls exactly
how days are counted, using the same day-count conventions as the
bond-pricing functions ([ACCRINT](/docs/financial/accrint),
[YIELD](/docs/financial/yield)) — 30/360 treats every month as having
30 days, while the actual/365 basis counts real calendar days against
a 365-day year.

<Callout type="warn">
  Changing `basis` can meaningfully change the result on the same two
  dates — it isn't just a rounding difference. From January 1 to July
  1, basis `0` (30/360) gives exactly `0.5`, while basis `3`
  (actual/365) gives `0.4959`, since that particular 6-month span
  actually spans 181 real days, not a clean 180.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Results don't match a different system's fraction-of-year calculation

<ExcelCode>{`YEARFRAC(start, end) ≠ a value computed elsewhere with actual/365 day counting`}</ExcelCode>

Not a bug — check which `basis` the comparison system uses. YEARFRAC
defaults to 30/360, which disagrees with an actual/365 calculation on
the same two dates by a small but real amount.

### Expecting a whole number instead of a fraction

<ExcelCode>{`YEARFRAC("1/1/2025", "1/1/2027") → 2.0 (a whole number is a valid result, not an error)`}</ExcelCode>

Not an error — a span of exactly whole years returns a clean whole
number, since that genuinely is the correct fraction (200% of a
year, expressed as `2.0`).

        d1 = min(start.day, 30)

        days = (end.year - start.year) * 360 + (end.month - start.month) * 30 + (d2 - d1)
