Replace a chunk of text at a known position and length — the
position-based counterpart to SUBSTITUTE, which replaces based on
matching text content instead.

## Return value

`old_text` with the `num_chars` characters starting at `start_num`
replaced by `new_text`. Everything outside that range is left
unchanged.

## Description

REPLACE swaps out a segment defined by position and length, without
caring what text is actually there — the opposite approach from
SUBSTITUTE, which matches by content and doesn't care where it sits.
Use REPLACE when you know exactly where the text to change sits;
use SUBSTITUTE when you know what it says.

<Callout type="warn">
  REPLACE's arguments are `(old_text, start_num, num_chars,
  new_text)` — the two numeric position arguments sit in the middle,
  unlike SUBSTITUTE's `(text, old_text, new_text)`, where everything
  is text. Mixing up the two functions' argument orders is a common
  source of errors.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Wrong characters replaced due to a miscounted position

<ExcelCode>{`REPLACE(A2, 5, 3, "XXX") → replaces the wrong segment if start_num is off`}</ExcelCode>

Double-check `start_num` by counting characters carefully, or use
FIND to locate the position dynamically instead of guessing a fixed
number.

### Confusing REPLACE's arguments with SUBSTITUTE's

<ExcelCode>{`REPLACE(A2, "abc", "xyz") → wrong — REPLACE needs numeric position arguments, not text`}</ExcelCode>

That call belongs to SUBSTITUTE, not REPLACE. REPLACE always needs a
numeric `start_num` and `num_chars` in the middle.

## Related functions

- [SUBSTITUTE](/docs/text/substitute) — replaces based on matching
  text content instead of a fixed position and length.
- [FIND](/docs/text/find) and [SEARCH](/docs/text/search) — often
  nested inside `start_num` to locate a dynamic replacement position.
- [MID](/docs/text/mid) — extracts a segment the same way REPLACE
  targets one, without replacing it.
