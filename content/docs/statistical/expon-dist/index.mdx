Return a value from the exponential distribution — the standard model
for the waiting time until the next event in a Poisson-style process,
like the time between support calls or between equipment failures.

## Return value

With `cumulative` `TRUE`: the probability that the waiting time until
the next event is `x` or less. With `cumulative` `FALSE`: the density
of that curve at `x`.

## Description

EXPON.DIST is the natural companion to `POISSON.DIST`: while
POISSON.DIST answers "how many events happen in a fixed interval,"
EXPON.DIST answers "how long until the next one happens," given the
same underlying constant event rate `lambda`. The distribution's mean
waiting time is `1 / lambda` — a `lambda` of 4 events per hour implies
an average wait of 15 minutes (`1/4` of an hour) between events.

<Callout type="idea">
  The exponential distribution is famously **memoryless**: the
  probability of waiting at least an additional `t` units, given that
  no event has happened yet, is exactly the same as the probability
  of waiting `t` units from the very start. Past waiting time gives no
  information about how much longer the wait will be — a property
  essentially no other common distribution shares.
</Callout>

A process averaging 0.25 events per unit time — a mean wait of `1 /
0.25 = 4` units between events. The chance of waiting 4 units or less
is 0.6321 (again `1 − 1/e`, the same landmark value seen on
`WEIBULL.DIST`'s page), and the chance of waiting 8 units or less —
double the mean wait — climbs to 0.8647.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — x is negative, or lambda isn't positive

<ExcelCode>{`EXPON.DIST(-1, 0.25, TRUE) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`x` must be non-negative, and `lambda` must be strictly positive.

### Confusing lambda (a rate) with the mean wait time itself

<ExcelCode>{`EXPON.DIST(4, 4, TRUE) → treats 4 as the rate (4 events per unit time), not a 4-unit mean wait`}</ExcelCode>

`lambda` is a rate — events per unit time — not the average wait
directly. If the known quantity is an average wait time (say, 4
minutes between calls), pass its reciprocal: `lambda = 1/4 = 0.25`.
