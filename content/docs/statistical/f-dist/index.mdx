## Return value

With `cumulative` `TRUE`: the probability that an F-distributed
random variable with `deg_freedom1` and `deg_freedom2` degrees of
freedom is less than or equal to `x`. With `cumulative` `FALSE`: the
density of that curve at `x`.

## Description

F.DIST is the direct way to turn an F-statistic (the kind `F.TEST`
computes internally as a ratio of two variances) into a left-tailed
probability. Unlike the symmetric normal or t-distributions, the
F-distribution only takes non-negative values, and its exact shape
depends on **both** degrees-of-freedom parameters — swapping
`deg_freedom1` and `deg_freedom2` changes the curve, unlike F.TEST's
own result, which stays the same either way.

<Callout type="warn">
  F.DIST is **left-tailed**, but `F.TEST` reports a two-tailed result
  built from the right tail. Use `F.DIST.RT`, not plain F.DIST, if the
  goal is reproducing the direction F.TEST's underlying statistic
  actually points in.
</Callout>

A variance-ratio statistic tested with 3 and 10 degrees of freedom.
At `x = 1` (equal variances), F.DIST returns 0.5677 — noticeably
above 0.5 because the F-distribution isn't symmetric, unlike the
normal or t-distributions. At `x = 3.71`, the standard 95%
critical value for this pair of degrees of freedom, F.DIST returns
almost exactly 0.9501.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — x is negative, or either deg_freedom is less than 1

<ExcelCode>{`F.DIST(-1, 3, 10, TRUE) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`x` must be non-negative, and both degrees-of-freedom arguments must
be at least 1.

### Swapping deg_freedom1 and deg_freedom2 by mistake

<ExcelCode>{`F.DIST(1, 3, 10, TRUE) → 0.5677, not the same as F.DIST(1, 10, 3, TRUE) → 0.4323`}</ExcelCode>

Unlike `F.TEST` (which returns the same two-tailed answer regardless
of argument order), F.DIST's result genuinely depends on which
degrees of freedom is the numerator and which is the denominator —
double-check they're in the right order for what `x` was actually
computed from.
