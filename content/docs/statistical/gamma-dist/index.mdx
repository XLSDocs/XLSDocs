Return a value from the gamma distribution — a flexible, always-
positive, right-skewed distribution commonly used for waiting times,
insurance claim sizes, and rainfall totals.

## Return value

With `cumulative` `TRUE`: the probability that a gamma-distributed
random variable with shape `alpha` and scale `beta` is less than or
equal to `x`. With `cumulative` `FALSE`: the density of that curve
at `x`.

## Description

The gamma distribution models positive, right-skewed quantities — the
total time until a fixed number of independent events occur, an
insurance claim's dollar amount, monthly rainfall. `alpha` (shape)
controls how skewed the curve is: small `alpha` gives a curve that
peaks near zero with a long right tail, while larger `alpha` shifts
the peak away from zero and makes the curve more symmetric. `beta`
(scale) simply stretches or compresses the whole curve without
changing its basic shape. The distribution's mean is `alpha × beta`.

<Callout type="idea">
  When `alpha = 1`, the gamma distribution reduces exactly to the
  exponential distribution with rate `1/beta` — `GAMMA.DIST(x, 1,
  beta, TRUE)` and `EXPON.DIST(x, 1/beta, TRUE)` return identical
  results. And when `alpha` is a positive integer, the gamma
  distribution describes the total waiting time for exactly that many
  independent exponential events to occur.
</Callout>

A gamma distribution with shape 2 and scale 3 — mean `2 × 3 = 6`. The
probability of a value of 6 or less is 0.5940, slightly above 50%
even though 6 is the mean, reflecting the distribution's right skew
(the long tail above the mean pulls the median below it).

## Common errors

### #NUM! — x is negative, or alpha/beta isn't positive

<ExcelCode>{`GAMMA.DIST(-1, 2, 3, TRUE) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`x` must be non-negative, and both `alpha` and `beta` must be
strictly positive.

### Expecting the median to equal alpha × beta

<ExcelCode>{`GAMMA.DIST(6, 2, 3, TRUE) → 0.5940, not 0.5, even though the mean is exactly 6`}</ExcelCode>

The gamma distribution's right skew means its median is generally
**below** its mean — `alpha × beta` gives the mean, not the 50th
percentile. Use `GAMMA.INV(0.5, alpha, beta)` to find the actual
median.
