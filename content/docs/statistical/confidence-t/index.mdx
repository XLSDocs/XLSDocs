Calculate the margin of error for estimating a population mean from a
sample, using the Student's t-distribution — better suited than
`CONFIDENCE.NORM` when the sample is small or the population standard
deviation isn't actually known.

## Return value

The margin of error, based on the Student's t-distribution with `size
− 1` degrees of freedom. The full confidence interval is the sample
mean plus or minus this value.

## Description

CONFIDENCE.T uses the Student's t-distribution instead of the normal
distribution `CONFIDENCE.NORM` relies on — the t-distribution has
fatter tails, which correctly accounts for the extra uncertainty that
comes from estimating the population standard deviation from a
limited sample rather than knowing it outright. As sample size grows
large, the t-distribution converges toward the normal distribution,
and CONFIDENCE.T's result converges toward CONFIDENCE.NORM's.

<Callout type="idea">
  CONFIDENCE.T's result is always **greater than or equal to**
  CONFIDENCE.NORM's on the same inputs — the t-distribution's fatter
  tails produce a wider (more conservative) margin of error,
  especially noticeable with a small sample size. The gap between
  them shrinks as `size` grows.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — alpha is outside the 0–1 range, or size is less than 2

<ExcelCode>{`CONFIDENCE.T(0.05, 5, 1) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`alpha` must be strictly between 0 and 1, and `size` must be at
least 2 — the t-distribution's degrees of freedom (`size − 1`) can't
be zero.

### Expecting an identical result to CONFIDENCE.NORM

<ExcelCode>{`CONFIDENCE.T(0.05, 5, 40) → 1.5991, not the same as CONFIDENCE.NORM(0.05, 5, 40) → 1.55`}</ExcelCode>

These use different underlying distributions and aren't meant to
match — the gap narrows as `size` increases but never fully
disappears for finite samples.
