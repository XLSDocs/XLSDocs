Return a value from the normal distribution for **any** mean and
standard deviation — either the cumulative probability up to a given
point, or the raw density there.

## Return value

With `cumulative` `TRUE`: the probability that a normally-distributed
random variable with the given `mean` and `standard_dev` is less than
or equal to `x`. With `cumulative` `FALSE`: the density of that curve
at `x`.

## Description

NORM.DIST is the general-purpose version of the normal distribution
calculation — unlike `NORM.S.DIST`, it doesn't assume mean `0` and
standard deviation `1`, so raw, real-scale data (test scores, heights,
measurement errors) can be plugged in directly without converting to
z-scores first.

<Callout type="idea">
  `NORM.DIST(x, mean, standard_dev, TRUE)` gives exactly the same
  result as `NORM.S.DIST((x − mean) / standard_dev, TRUE)` — NORM.DIST
  just performs that standardization internally, saving a separate
  `STANDARDIZE` step.
</Callout>

IQ scores are modeled as normal with a mean of 100 and a standard
deviation of 15. A score of 115 (one standard deviation above the
mean) sits at the 84th percentile — matching the familiar "68% within
1 SD" rule, where roughly 16% falls above 115.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — standard_dev is zero or negative

<ExcelCode>{`NORM.DIST(100, 100, 0, TRUE) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`standard_dev` must be a positive number — a distribution with zero
or negative spread isn't meaningful.

### Forgetting to convert a percentage-style mean/standard_dev consistently

<ExcelCode>{`NORM.DIST(1.1, 1, 0.05, TRUE) → treats 1.1 as a raw value, matching a mean of 1 and sd of 0.05`}</ExcelCode>

`x`, `mean`, and `standard_dev` all need to be on the same scale (all
raw values, or all percentages, etc.) — mixing a percentage `x` with
a raw-number `mean` produces a meaningless result without erroring.
