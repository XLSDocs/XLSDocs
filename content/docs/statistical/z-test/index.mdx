Test whether a sample's mean is significantly different from a
hypothesized value `x`, when the population standard deviation is
known — returns a one-tailed probability, not a raw z-score.

## Return value

A probability between 0 and 1 — the one-tailed likelihood of
observing a sample mean at least this far above `x` by chance alone,
if the true population mean actually equals `x`.

## Description

Z.TEST computes `z = (mean(array) − x) / (sigma / SQRT(n))`, then
returns `1 − NORM.S.DIST(z, TRUE)` — the area under the standard
normal curve to the right of that z-score. A result close to 0 means
the sample mean sits far enough above `x` that it's unlikely to have
happened by chance; a result close to 1 means the sample mean is at
or below `x`, giving no evidence the true mean exceeds it.

<Callout type="warn">
  Z.TEST is built for a **one-tailed, "is the mean greater than x"**
  question. For a two-tailed test (mean is different from `x`, in
  either direction), double the smaller of Z.TEST's result and `1 −`
  its result: `=2 * MIN(Z.TEST(array, x, sigma), 1 - Z.TEST(array, x, sigma))`.
  Also note Excel's own docs describe Z.TEST as most useful with
  `sigma` supplied — with a small sample, estimating both the mean and
  standard deviation from the same data makes `T.TEST` the more
  standard choice.
</Callout>

Five test scores (mean 90.2) tested against a known population

returns 0.0100 — strong evidence the sample mean is really above 85.
Against 90 (almost exactly the sample mean), it returns 0.4644 — no
real evidence either way. Against 92 (above the sample mean), it
returns 0.7896, since the sample gives no support for a mean that
high.

## Common errors

### #N/A — array contains no numeric values

<ExcelCode>{`Z.TEST(A1:A5, 90) → #N/A when A1:A5 has no numbers`}</ExcelCode>

`array` needs at least one numeric value to compute a mean and
standard deviation from.

### Misreading the result as a two-tailed p-value

<ExcelCode>{`Z.TEST(array, x, sigma) → one-tailed only, not directly comparable to a two-tailed T.TEST result`}</ExcelCode>

Z.TEST's raw output answers "is the mean greater than x," not "is the
mean different from x." Double the smaller tail (see the callout
above) before comparing it to a two-tailed significance threshold like
0.05.
