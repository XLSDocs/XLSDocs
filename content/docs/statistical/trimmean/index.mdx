Calculate an average that ignores the most extreme values at both the
top and bottom of a dataset — a way to reduce the influence of
outliers without deciding by hand which data points to throw out.

## Return value

The arithmetic mean of `array` after excluding the specified
percentage of the highest and lowest values.

## Description

TRIMMEAN sorts `array`, calculates how many data points `percent`
represents, rounds that count *down* to the nearest even number (so it
splits evenly between the top and bottom), removes that many points
from each extreme, and averages what's left. With 10 data points and
`percent` of `0.2`, that's 20% of 10 = 2 points total excluded — one
from the top, one from the bottom — leaving 8 values to average.

<Callout type="idea">
  TRIMMEAN is a middle ground between AVERAGE (uses every value,
  fully exposed to outliers) and MEDIAN (ignores almost everything
  except the middle value). A small `percent` like `0.1` or `0.2`
  keeps most of the data while still discounting a handful of extreme
  scores — a common choice for judged competitions, where the highest
  and lowest scores are conventionally dropped.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — percent is out of range

<ExcelCode>{`TRIMMEAN(A1:A10, 1.5) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`percent` must be between 0 and 1. A value of `1` or greater would
attempt to trim 100% or more of the data, leaving nothing to average.

### #VALUE! — array contains no numbers

<ExcelCode>{`TRIMMEAN("text", 0.2) → #VALUE!`}</ExcelCode>

`array` needs to be a range or array of actual numeric values.
