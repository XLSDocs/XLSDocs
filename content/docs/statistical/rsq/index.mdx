Return the R-squared value of a linear regression between two
datasets — the proportion of variance in one variable that's
explained by the other, from `0` (no linear relationship) to `1`
(perfect fit).

## Return value

The square of the Pearson correlation coefficient between
`known_ys` and `known_xs` — a value from `0` to `1`.

## Description

RSQ answers "how much of the variation in y is explained by x?" — an
RSQ of `0.90` means 90% of the variation in `known_ys` can be
accounted for by its linear relationship with `known_xs`, leaving
10% unexplained (noise, or factors outside the model). It's simply
`CORREL` squared, which is also why RSQ is always positive even when
the underlying relationship is negative (a strong negative
correlation still means a strong, high-RSQ fit).

<Callout type="idea">
  Squaring a correlation always removes its sign, which is exactly
  why RSQ can't tell you whether the relationship is positive or
  negative — only how *strong* the linear fit is. Check `CORREL` or
  `SLOPE` directly if the direction of the relationship matters.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #DIV/0! — known_xs or known_ys has no variance

<ExcelCode>{`RSQ({5,5,5}, {1,2,3}) → #DIV/0!`}</ExcelCode>

If every value in either range is identical, there's no variation to
explain a relationship with.

### Expecting RSQ to reveal the direction of the relationship

<ExcelCode>{`RSQ always ≥ 0, even for a strong NEGATIVE correlation`}</ExcelCode>

RSQ can't distinguish a strong positive relationship from a strong
negative one — both square to a similarly high value. Check `CORREL`
or `SLOPE` for the sign.
