Return a value from the Student's t-distribution — either the
left-tailed cumulative probability up to a given t-value, or the raw
density there.

## Return value

With `cumulative` `TRUE`: the probability that a t-distributed random
variable with `deg_freedom` degrees of freedom is less than or equal
to `x` — the area under the curve to the left of `x`. With
`cumulative` `FALSE`: the density of that curve at `x`.

## Description

T.DIST is the direct way to turn a t-statistic (the kind `T.TEST`
computes internally) into a left-tailed probability. The
t-distribution looks like the standard normal curve but with fatter
tails — the smaller `deg_freedom` is, the fatter those tails are, and
the more T.DIST's result differs from what `NORM.S.DIST` would give
on the same input. As `deg_freedom` grows large, the two converge.

<Callout type="warn">
  T.DIST is **left-tailed**, unlike the legacy `TDIST` function, which
  was always right-tailed (and two-tailed only) — porting an old
  `TDIST` formula to T.DIST isn't a drop-in swap. For a right-tailed
  result, use `T.DIST.RT`; for the classic two-tailed p-value a
  hypothesis test usually needs, use `T.DIST.2T`.
</Callout>

A t-statistic computed from a sample of 13 observations (12 degrees
of freedom). At `t = 2`, T.DIST returns 0.9657 — 96.57% of the
distribution's area sits to the left of 2. As `t` climbs toward 3,
the result creeps closer to 1, since fewer and fewer outcomes lie
further left.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — deg_freedom is less than 1

<ExcelCode>{`T.DIST(2, 0, TRUE) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`deg_freedom` must be at least 1 — a t-distribution needs at least one
degree of freedom to be defined.

### Expecting a two-tailed result from T.DIST directly

<ExcelCode>{`T.DIST(2, 12, TRUE) → 0.9657, not a p-value ready to compare against 0.05`}</ExcelCode>

T.DIST's raw result is the left tail only. For the two-tailed p-value
a typical hypothesis test compares against a 0.05 threshold, use
`T.DIST.2T` instead, or compute `2 * MIN(T.DIST(x, df, TRUE), 1 -
T.DIST(x, df, TRUE))`.
