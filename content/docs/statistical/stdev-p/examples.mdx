---
title: STDEV.P examples
description: Worked examples for STDEV.P — full-batch quality data, class-wide test scores, and comparing to STDEV.S.
---

<FunctionNav />

<ExampleCard number="01" label="Basic — spread across an entire batch" formula={`=STDEV.P(FullBatch[Weight])`}>
  Returns the population standard deviation when every unit produced
  is included, not a sample of them.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="02" label="Every student's score on a single test" formula={`=STDEV.P(ClassScores[Value])`}>
  Valid as a population calculation only when the range covers every
  student who took the test — the whole population, not a subset.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="03" label="Comparing population vs sample" formula={`=STDEV.P(Data[Value]) - STDEV.S(Data[Value])`}>
  Shows that STDEV.P is always less than or equal to STDEV.S on
  identical data — the result here is always zero or negative.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="04" label="Population coefficient of variation" formula={`=STDEV.P(Readings[Value]) / AVERAGE(Readings[Value])`}>
  Spread relative to the mean, using the population version — useful
  when comparing complete datasets of different scales.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="05" label="Z-score using population parameters" formula={`=(A2 - AVERAGE(Data[Value])) / STDEV.P(Data[Value])`}>
  Standardizes a value into units of population standard deviation
  from the mean.
</ExampleCard>
