Return the probability of a given number of events occurring within a
fixed interval of time or space, given a known long-run average rate
— call volume in an hour, defects per meter of cable, customer
arrivals in a day.

## Return value

With `cumulative` `TRUE`: the probability of `x` or fewer events
occurring. With `cumulative` `FALSE`: the probability of exactly `x`
events occurring.

## Description

POISSON.DIST models events that happen independently and at a
constant average rate, with no natural upper limit on how many could
occur — unlike the binomial distribution's fixed number of trials.
`mean` (often written `λ`, lambda) can be any positive number, even
though `x` itself must always be a whole count of events.

<Callout type="idea">
  The Poisson distribution is often used as an approximation to the
  binomial distribution when `trials` is large and `probability_s` is
  small — `mean = trials × probability_s` in that case. This is why
  rare-event counts (typos per page, defects per batch) are so often
  modeled with Poisson even though the underlying process is really a
  huge number of low-probability binomial trials.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — x or mean is negative

<ExcelCode>{`POISSON.DIST(-1, 4, TRUE) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`x` must be a non-negative integer, and `mean` must be a positive
number.

### Using POISSON.DIST when events aren't actually independent

<ExcelCode>{`POISSON.DIST(10, 4, TRUE) → assumes each event's timing is unaffected by the others`}</ExcelCode>

If one event happening makes another more or less likely soon after
(support calls spiking right after an outage, for example), the
Poisson assumption of independent events at a constant rate breaks
down and the result won't reflect the true probability.
