## Return value

With `cumulative` `TRUE`: the probability that a chi-square-distributed
random variable with `deg_freedom` degrees of freedom is less than or
equal to `x`. With `cumulative` `FALSE`: the density of that curve
at `x`.

## Description

CHISQ.DIST is the direct way to turn a chi-square statistic (the kind
`CHISQ.TEST` computes internally from observed-vs-expected counts)
into a left-tailed probability. Unlike the symmetric normal or
t-distributions, the chi-square distribution only takes non-negative
values and is right-skewed — more so at low degrees of freedom, and
increasingly bell-shaped as `deg_freedom` grows.

<Callout type="warn">
  CHISQ.DIST is **left-tailed**, but `CHISQ.TEST` itself works off the
  **right** tail — a large chi-square statistic (evidence the
  observed and expected counts really differ) corresponds to a
  *small* right-tail probability. Use `CHISQ.DIST.RT`, not plain
  CHISQ.DIST, to reproduce the p-value shape CHISQ.TEST returns.
</Callout>

that point.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — x is negative, or deg_freedom is less than 1

<ExcelCode>{`CHISQ.DIST(-2, 4, TRUE) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`x` must be non-negative and `deg_freedom` must be at least 1 — the
chi-square distribution is only defined for non-negative values.

### Expecting CHISQ.DIST to match CHISQ.TEST's p-value directly

<ExcelCode>{`CHISQ.DIST(9.488, 4, TRUE) → 0.9500, not the 0.05 p-value CHISQ.TEST would report on the same statistic`}</ExcelCode>

CHISQ.TEST reports a right-tailed probability — the chance of a
statistic *at least this large*. Use `1 - CHISQ.DIST(x, df, TRUE)`,
or more directly `CHISQ.DIST.RT(x, df)`, to match it.
