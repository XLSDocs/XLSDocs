Predict values along an **exponential** trend fitted through
historical data — TREND's counterpart for data that compounds
(percentage growth) rather than changes by a fixed amount each
period.

## Return value

The predicted `y` value(s) at `new_xs`, based on the best-fit
exponential curve (`y = b × mˣ`) through `known_xs` and `known_ys`.

## Description

GROWTH is `TREND`'s counterpart for data that compounds rather than
changes by a fixed amount each period — revenue growing by a
consistent percentage, population growth, compound interest. It fits
the curve `y = b × mˣ` (rather than TREND's straight line `y = mx +
b`) by taking the logarithm of `known_ys`, fitting a straight line to
that, then converting back.

<Callout type="warn">
  Every value in `known_ys` must be **positive** — GROWTH takes their
  logarithm internally, and the logarithm of zero or a negative
  number is undefined. Using GROWTH on data that includes a zero or a
  loss (negative value) returns `#NUM!`.
</Callout>

Revenue growing by exactly 10% every month. GROWTH correctly
continues that same 10% compounding pace: month 5 predicts 146.41
(133.10 × 1.1), and month 6 predicts 161.05 — a plain linear `TREND`
on the same data would predict smaller, understated values, since it
would assume a fixed dollar increase instead of a fixed percentage
one.

## Common errors

### #NUM! — known_ys contains a zero or negative value

<ExcelCode>{`GROWTH({100, 0, 121}, {1,2,3}) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

GROWTH works by taking the logarithm of `known_ys` internally — a
zero or negative value has no logarithm, so it breaks the whole
calculation.

### Using GROWTH on data that's actually linear

<ExcelCode>{`GROWTH on a fixed +10-per-period series overstates later predictions compared to TREND`}</ExcelCode>

If the data increases by a constant *amount* each period rather than
a constant *percentage*, `TREND` is the correct fit — GROWTH will
compound growth that isn't actually happening at a compounding rate.

### #SPILL! — blocked spill range

When `new_xs` has more than one value, GROWTH spills its results —
clear the cells in the spill direction, or move the formula
somewhere with room.
