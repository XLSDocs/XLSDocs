Round a number away from zero — always up in magnitude, even if the
value is much closer to the lower number. The function behind "round
up to the next whole unit" logic like packaging, billing increments,
or capacity planning.

## Return value

`number` rounded away from zero to `num_digits` digits — the result
is always at least as large in magnitude as `number`, never smaller.

## Description

Unlike ROUND, which picks whichever direction is closer, ROUNDUP
always moves away from zero — `ROUNDUP(3.01, 0)` gives `4`, not `3`,
even though `3.01` is much closer to `3`. This makes it the right
tool whenever "round up no matter what" is the actual business
rule — how many boxes are needed to ship a given quantity, or how
many billing periods a partial period counts as.

<Callout type="warn">
  ROUNDUP rounds **away from zero** for negative numbers too —
  `ROUNDUP(-3.01, 0)` gives `-4`, not `-3`. "Away from zero" means
  larger in magnitude, not necessarily numerically larger.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Expecting "round up" to mean "round toward positive infinity"

<ExcelCode>{`ROUNDUP(-3.01, 0) → -4 (not -3)`}</ExcelCode>

ROUNDUP rounds away from zero, not toward positive infinity — for
negative numbers, that means a more negative result. If rounding
toward positive infinity specifically is needed regardless of sign,
use CEILING instead.

### Using ROUND when the business rule actually needs ROUNDUP

<ExcelCode>{`ROUND(3.01, 0) → 3 (rounds to the closer value, not away from zero)`}</ExcelCode>

If the rule is genuinely "always round up, even by a fraction," ROUND
is the wrong function — it rounds to whichever value is closer, which
usually isn't up.
