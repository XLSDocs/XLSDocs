---
title: MAXIFS examples
description: Worked examples for MAXIFS — basic matching, multiple conditions, and combining with MAX.
---

<FunctionNav />

<ExampleCard number="01" label="Basic — largest sale by region" formula={`=MAXIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], "West")`}>
  Returns the largest amount among rows where Region equals "West".
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="02" label="Multiple conditions" formula={`=MAXIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], "West", Sales[Quarter], "Q4")`}>
  Both conditions must be true (AND logic) — West region **and** Q4.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="03" label="Latest deadline among open projects" formula={`=MAXIFS(Projects[DueDate], Projects[Status], "Open")`}>
  MAXIFS works on dates too, since Excel stores them as numbers —
  this finds the furthest-out due date among open projects.
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="04" label="OR logic via two calls" formula={`=MAX(MAXIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], "West"), MAXIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], "East"))`}>
  MAXIFS alone only ANDs conditions — wrapping two calls in MAX
  answers "the largest sale in West OR East."
</ExampleCard>

<ExampleCard number="05" label="Dynamic criteria from a cell" formula={`=MAXIFS(Sales[Amount], Sales[Region], D2)`}>
  Referencing a cell instead of hardcoding "West" lets the same
  formula answer a different question when D2 changes.
</ExampleCard>
