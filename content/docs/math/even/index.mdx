Round a number up to the nearest even integer — always moving away
from zero, never toward it. `EVEN(3)` gives `4`; `EVEN(-3)` gives
`-4`, not `-2`.

## Return value

`number` rounded up to the nearest even integer, always moving away
from zero. Returns `number` unchanged if it's already an even
integer.

## Description

EVEN always rounds **away from zero**, not toward positive infinity
— that distinction only shows up for negative numbers, but it's the
detail that trips people up. `EVEN(3)` becomes `4` (moving up, away
from zero), and `EVEN(-3)` becomes `-4` (also moving away from zero,
which for a negative number means getting *more* negative, not
closer to zero).

<Callout type="info">
  "Away from zero" and "toward positive infinity" are the same
  direction for positive numbers, which is exactly why the
  distinction is easy to miss. They diverge for negative numbers:
  `EVEN(-3)` moves away from zero to `-4`, not toward positive
  infinity to `-2`.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Expecting EVEN to round negative numbers toward zero

<ExcelCode>{`EVEN(-3) → -4 (not -2 — EVEN moves away from zero, not toward positive infinity)`}</ExcelCode>

If a value closer to zero was expected here, remember EVEN's
direction is always "away from zero" for both positive and negative
inputs — it doesn't switch behavior based on sign the way some
rounding functions do.

### Assuming EVEN always changes the value

<ExcelCode>{`EVEN(4) → 4 (unchanged — 4 is already even)`}</ExcelCode>

Not an error — an input that's already an even integer is returned
exactly as-is, with no rounding applied.
