Round `number` up to the nearest integer or multiple of
`significance`, always rounding toward positive infinity — negative
numbers included — regardless of the sign of either argument.

## Return value

`number` rounded up to the nearest multiple of `significance`,
always moving toward positive infinity. `CEILING.PRECISE(4.3, 2)`
returns `6`; `CEILING.PRECISE(-4.3)` returns `-4`.

## Description

CEILING.PRECISE solves the sign headache that CEILING has: instead
of requiring `number` and `significance` to share a sign, it always
rounds toward positive infinity, no matter what sign either argument
has. For a negative `number`, that means rounding *toward* zero
(`-4.3` becomes `-4`, not `-6`) — the opposite of what CEILING.MATH
does by default.

<ExcelCode>{`=CEILING.PRECISE(4.3, 2) → 6 (next multiple of 2 at or above 4.3)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="info">
  **CEILING.PRECISE behaves identically to ISO.CEILING** — both
  always round toward positive infinity and ignore the sign of
  `significance`. ISO.CEILING exists specifically for compatibility
  with the Open Document Format (ODF) spreadsheet standard; pick
  whichever name reads better in context.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Zero result from a zero argument

<ExcelCode>{`CEILING.PRECISE(0, 5) → 0`}</ExcelCode>
<ExcelCode>{`CEILING.PRECISE(4.3, 0) → 0`}</ExcelCode>

If either `number` or `significance` is `0`, the result is `0` —
there's no non-zero multiple to round a zero value to.

## Related functions

  added for ODF compatibility.
- [CEILING.MATH](/docs/math/ceiling-math) — adds a `mode` argument
  to control negative-number rounding direction, rather than always
  rounding toward positive infinity.
- [FLOOR.PRECISE](/docs/math/floor-precise) — the rounding-down
  counterpart, always rounding toward negative infinity.
