Return the **hyperbolic cosine** of a number — the exponential-based
analogue of ordinary cosine, best known for describing the shape of
a hanging cable or chain (a catenary curve) rather than anything to
do with triangles or angles.

## Return value

The hyperbolic cosine of `number` — always `1` or greater, regardless
of whether `number` is positive or negative, with the minimum value
of exactly `1` occurring at `number = 0`.

## Description

COSH is defined by the formula `(e^x + e^-x) / 2`, built from the
exponential function rather than from a circle the way ordinary
cosine is. It's most famous as the shape of a catenary curve — the
natural curve a chain or cable forms when hanging freely under its
own weight, which is literally a graph of COSH. It's also an even
function (symmetric around zero): `COSH(x)` and `COSH(-x)` always
give the same result.

<Callout type="idea">
  **COSH has nothing to do with angles.** Unlike COS, there's no
  radians-vs-degrees conversion to worry about — `number` is just an
  ordinary real value, plugged directly into the exponential formula
  above.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Assuming COSH needs an angle in radians

<ExcelCode>{`COSH(RADIANS(60)) → not what you want — COSH takes a plain number, not an angle`}</ExcelCode>

COSH's argument is an ordinary number, not an angle — converting it
with RADIANS first (a habit carried over from COS) changes the input
to something unrelated to what was intended.

### #VALUE! — a non-numeric argument

<ExcelCode>{`COSH("four") → #VALUE!`}</ExcelCode>

`number` must be a value Excel can read as numeric.
