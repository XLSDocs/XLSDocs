Round `number` to the *nearest* multiple of `multiple` — unlike
CEILING or FLOOR, which always round in one fixed direction, MROUND
rounds to whichever multiple is closer.

## Return value

`number` rounded to the nearest multiple of `multiple`.
`MROUND(10, 3)` returns `9` (closer to 9 than to 12);
`MROUND(1.3, 0.2)` returns `1.4`.

## Description

MROUND fills the gap between CEILING and FLOOR: instead of always
rounding up or always rounding down, it picks whichever multiple of
`multiple` is actually closer to `number`. It's the right tool
whenever "round to the nearest X" is the goal, rather than a
guaranteed direction.

<ExcelCode>{`=MROUND(10, 3) → 9 (9 is 1 away, 12 is 2 away — 9 wins)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="warn">
  **`number` and `multiple` must share the same sign** —
  `MROUND(5, -2)` returns `#NUM!`, since a positive number can't be
  rounded to a negative multiple. This is stricter than CEILING.MATH
  or FLOOR.MATH, which both allow mismatched signs.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — mismatched signs

<ExcelCode>{`MROUND(5, -2) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

`number` and `multiple` need matching signs — use `MROUND(-5, -2)`
for a negative number.

## Related functions

- [CEILING.MATH](/docs/math/ceiling-math) — always rounds up to a
  multiple, rather than to the nearest one.
- [FLOOR.MATH](/docs/math/floor-math) — always rounds down to a
  multiple, rather than to the nearest one.
- [ROUND](/docs/math/round) — rounds to a number of decimal digits,
  rather than to an arbitrary multiple.
