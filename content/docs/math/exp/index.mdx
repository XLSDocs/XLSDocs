## Return value

`e` raised to `number`. A negative `number` returns the reciprocal of
`e` raised to its absolute value — `EXP(-1)` is about `0.368`, the
reciprocal of `EXP(1)`.

## Description

EXP computes `e^number`, where `e` is the base of the natural
logarithm — a mathematical constant that shows up throughout
continuous growth and decay calculations, compound interest, and
calculus. It's the direct counterpart to LN: whatever LN undoes, EXP
redoes.

<ExcelCode>{`=EXP(1) → 2.718281828 (e itself, to nine decimal places)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="info">
  **To raise a different base to a power**, use the `^` operator or
  POWER instead — EXP is specifically base-`e`. `EXP(3)` computes
  `e^3`, not `10^3` or any other base; for that, use `10^3` or
  `POWER(10, 3)`.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #VALUE! — a non-numeric argument

<ExcelCode>{`EXP("abc") → #VALUE!`}</ExcelCode>

`number` must be a number, a cell containing one, or a formula that
returns one.

## Related functions

- [LN](/docs/math/ln) — the inverse of EXP, returning the natural
  logarithm of a number.
- [LOG](/docs/math/log) and [LOG10](/docs/math/log10) — logarithms to
  a specified base or to base 10, rather than the natural log base.
- [POWER](/docs/math/power) — raises any base (not just e) to a given
  power.
