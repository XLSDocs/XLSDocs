## Return value

The natural logarithm of `number` — the exponent that `e` would need
to be raised to, to produce `number`.

## Description

LN answers the question "e to what power equals this number?" It's
the base-e counterpart of LOG10 (base 10) and the general-base LOG
function, and it's the inverse of EXP: raising e to the result LN
gives you back gets you to exactly where you started.

<ExcelCode>{`=LN(86) → 4.4543473 (rounded to 7 decimal places)`}</ExcelCode>

<Callout type="warn">
  **LN requires a positive number.** Zero or a negative argument
  returns `#NUM!` — the natural logarithm is mathematically undefined
  for non-positive values.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #NUM! — a zero or negative argument

<ExcelCode>{`LN(0) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>
<ExcelCode>{`LN(-5) → #NUM!`}</ExcelCode>

The natural logarithm is only defined for positive real numbers.

### #VALUE! — a non-numeric argument

<ExcelCode>{`LN("abc") → #VALUE!`}</ExcelCode>

`number` must be a number, a cell containing one, or a formula that
returns one.
