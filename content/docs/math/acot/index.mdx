Return the **arccotangent** — the angle whose cotangent is the given
number. `number` can be any real value, and the result comes back
in **radians**, strictly between `0` and `π` — a different principal
range from [ATAN](/docs/math/atan)'s.

## Return value

The angle, in radians, whose cotangent is `number`, always strictly
between `0` and `π` (0° to 180°, exclusive of both ends). `ACOT(0)`
returns exactly `π/2`.

## Description

ACOT is the inverse of [COT](/docs/math/cot) — given a cotangent
ratio, it finds the angle. Its principal range, `0` to `π`, is
different from every other basic inverse trig function: ASIN and
ATAN both use `-π/2` to `π/2`, while ACOS uses `0` to `π` — ACOT
matches ACOS's range, not ATAN's, despite being TAN's inverse rather
than COS's.

<Callout type="warn">
  **ACOT(number) is not the same as ATAN(1/number).** They agree for
  positive `number`, but diverge for negative values: `ATAN(1/-2)`
  gives a negative angle (about `-26.57°`), while `ACOT(-2)` gives a
  positive one (about `153.43°`) to stay inside its `0`-to-`π`
  range. Excel's ACOT applies a correction specifically to keep
  every result in that range, regardless of sign.
</Callout>

## Common errors

### Assuming ACOT(x) equals ATAN(1/x)

<ExcelCode>{`ACOT(-2) → 153.43° in degrees, not ATAN(1/-2)'s -26.57°`}</ExcelCode>

The two formulas only agree for positive `x`. For negative `x`,
ACOT adds `π` to keep the result inside its `0`-to-`π` range, while
a plain `ATAN(1/x)` would return a negative angle instead.

### Forgetting to convert the result to degrees

<ExcelCode>{`ACOT(1) → 0.7854 (radians, not the 45 degrees someone may expect)`}</ExcelCode>

ACOT's result is always in radians. Wrap it in `DEGREES()`, or
multiply by `180/PI()`, to get a degree value.
