Estimate the sample variance of a numeric column, restricted to the
rows that satisfy conditions in a **separate criteria range** —
DSUM's variance counterpart, treating the matched records as a
sample of a larger population.

## Return value

The sample variance of `field` across every record in `database`
that matches every condition in `criteria`.

## Description

DVAR shares DSUM's criteria-range mechanic exactly — same-row
conditions are AND, separate-row conditions are OR — but measures how
spread out the matching values are instead of totaling them. Like
`VAR.S`, it divides by one less than the number of matching records
(`n - 1`), which is the correct adjustment when those records are
treated as a *sample* representing a larger population rather than
the entire population itself.

<Callout type="warn">
  DVAR needs **at least 2** matching records — with exactly 1 match,
  `n - 1` is `0`, and the calculation divides by zero. Use `DVARP`
  instead if the matching records should be treated as the entire
  population rather than a sample (it divides by `n`, so it works
  even with a single match).
</Callout>

## Common errors

### #DIV/0! — only one record matches criteria

<ExcelCode>{`DVAR(A1:D10, "Amount", F1:G2) → #DIV/0!`}</ExcelCode>

DVAR's sample-variance formula divides by `n - 1` matching records.
With exactly one match, that's division by zero. Either broaden the
criteria to include more records, or switch to `DVARP` if a single
record legitimately represents the entire population being measured.

### The result seems too large compared to the data's scale

Variance is expressed in **squared** units — a field of dollar
amounts produces a variance in squared dollars, which is why the
number often looks disproportionately large next to the original
values. Take the square root (or use `DSTDEV` directly) to get a
result back in the original units.
